Research

My research takes a holistic approach to perception, planning, control, and system design to enable reliable and robust robot operation in challenging real-world environments. I aim to develop robots that can assist people or take over tedious and dangerous tasks, from inspecting high-voltage electrical substations, wind turbines, and tall bridges to exploring deep caves and mines and supporting search-and-rescue operations in disaster-stricken areas.

Uncertainty-aware informative path planning using deep neural networks

ORACLE is a library of deep learning methods that enable collision-free aerial navigation without an environment map or an estimate of the robot’s position, while supporting robust transfer from simulation to the real world. ORACLE enables safe, uncertainty-aware flight; its visually attentive variant, A-ORACLE, adds implicit information sampling. A further variant, seVAE-ORACLE, introduces a modular architecture that supports partial training on synthetic data and, when available, real-world data.

Vision-Language Navigation

Together with colleagues at VinRobotics, I recently explored vision-language models (VLMs) for semantic navigation in humanoid robots. We proposed a data-efficient method that enhances a VLM backbone with two complementary auxiliary representations.

First, explicit landmark tokens follow the ordered landmarks specified by the instruction and are trained to predict the future landmark region in both semantic 2D patch-feature space and viewpoint-dependent 3D geometric feature space. Second, an implicit future token learns to distinguish the landmark state that is actually reached from plausible same-scene counterfactual futures generated by a video world model.

Control and aerial manipulation

The Aerial Robotic Chain (ARC) is a reconfigurable platform composed of multiple linked micro aerial vehicles. It can change shape to traverse narrow passages, exert significant forces and torques, and carry substantial payloads. Its modular architecture also creates opportunities for distributed sensing and processing and for extending the system with additional vehicles.

We designed a hybrid controller for ARC and analyzed its stability in both free-flight and aerial manipulation modes [J4, C16]. We also proposed shape and motion planning methods for this multi-linked aerial robot system [C15].

My control research also includes extensive work on model predictive control for micro aerial vehicles, including a survey of the field [C9].

Resilient perception and field autonomy

I contributed to complementary multimodal sensor fusion for robust localization in degraded subterranean environments [C14]. At VinRobotics, I led SLAM development for humanoid robots in large-scale, dynamic industrial environments.

My field robotics work spans collision-tolerant aerial platforms, autonomous exploration, and infrastructure inspection [C1, C6, C10, C19, C20].

Multi-robot exploration and resilient robotic systems

I contributed to collaborative exploration with legged and aerial robots [C4, C7] and to a self-deployed wireless communications system for subterranean robots [C18]. As a member of Team CERBERUS, I was co-responsible for the autonomy stack of collision-tolerant aerial robots and developed LiDAR–camera calibration software.

My collaborative work with P. De Petris includes collision-tolerant aerial platforms and navigation in confined environments [C5, C6, C11, C13].

Building on this experience, I aim to investigate collision-tolerant robot design through the joint development of mechanical structure, perception, and control, enabling robots to withstand contact and continue operating in confined and cluttered environments.
